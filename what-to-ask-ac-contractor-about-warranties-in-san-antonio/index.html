What should an AC warranty cover?

An AC warranty is a set of written terms describing which parts or work are covered, for how long, and what the customer must do to keep coverage valid. A new system may have manufacturer coverage for specified equipment parts, while the installing contractor may offer separate coverage for labor or workmanship. Those are different promises, so ask to see both rather than relying on the word “warranty” by itself.

For a repair, ask whether the warranty applies to the replacement part, the labor, or both, and how long it lasts. Ask what happens if the same fault returns, and whether a follow-up visit is included. The answer should match the written terms and the work shown on your invoice.

How do manufacturer and contractor warranties differ?

A manufacturer warranty generally covers eligible equipment parts under the manufacturer’s conditions. It may not pay for diagnosis, labor, refrigerant, or other costs involved in replacing a part. A contractor warranty usually concerns the installation or the contractor’s repair work, but its coverage and duration depend on that provider’s written policy.

Ask who handles each step if something fails. Find out whether the contractor contacts the manufacturer for you, what documents you need to keep, and whether you pay for a service visit while a claim is reviewed. A clear answer helps you understand both the coverage and any costs that could remain your responsibility.

What will a local professional check before discussing coverage?

A technician should identify the equipment and the issue before saying whether a warranty may apply. For an existing system, that can include checking the model and serial numbers, reviewing the repair history, and looking for signs that help explain the failure. For a new installation, the professional should explain what equipment is being installed and how the installation and warranty paperwork will be documented.

During hot weather, clear scheduling and service terms can be especially useful when a system is down. Ask what the contractor can confirm during the visit and what depends on the manufacturer’s review. A diagnosis is not the same as warranty approval, and a responsible provider should not promise an outcome before the terms and facts are checked.

What can change the cost or timing of warranty work?

A warranty does not always mean a repair is free. The price you pay can depend on whether the failed item is covered, whether labor is included, and whether diagnosis, travel, refrigerant, or other materials fall outside the terms. Ask for an itemized explanation of any charge before approving the work, especially if a claim is still being reviewed.

Timing can depend on how quickly the problem is diagnosed, whether a covered part is available, and how long the manufacturer takes to confirm eligibility. The contractor should explain what can be done now, what must wait for approval or a part, and whether there is an option to proceed as a paid repair instead. Do not assume that a warranty claim will move at the same pace as an ordinary repair.

For a replacement, ask whether the proposal includes the equipment, installation labor, startup checks, and warranty registration steps. If the scope involves work that may require local approval or a permit, ask the contractor to verify what applies to the job and who is responsible for handling it.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which parts of this repair or installation are covered by the manufacturer, and which are covered by your company?
  • Does the warranty include labor, diagnosis, refrigerant, and the return visit if the problem comes back?
  • How long does each part of the coverage last, and when does the coverage period begin?
  • Do I need to register the equipment or complete maintenance to keep the warranty valid? Who handles registration?
  • What conditions or exclusions could void coverage, including work by another provider or a change in equipment use?
  • If a claim is denied, what will I owe for the diagnosis, parts, and labor?
  • Who should I contact for a claim, and what paperwork should I keep?
  • Can you put the coverage, exclusions, and any charges in the written estimate or invoice?

Listen for specific answers tied to the proposed work, not broad statements like “it’s covered.” If a term is unclear, ask the contractor to point it out in the written documents before you approve the job.

What warranty mistakes should you avoid?

Do not treat a manufacturer parts warranty as if it automatically covers labor. Do not assume every part, failure, or service visit qualifies, either. The warranty document controls, and coverage can depend on the equipment, the type of failure, and whether required conditions were met.

Keep the estimate, invoice, model and serial number information, and any registration confirmation or maintenance records. Avoid authorizing a repair based only on a verbal promise, and ask before another company works on equipment that may still be under warranty. If you are unsure whether a condition applies, ask the contractor to explain it before work begins.

What is the next step?

Gather any existing warranty paperwork and write down the system’s model and serial numbers if they are available. Ask the contractor to diagnose the issue, explain which coverage may apply, and give you the expected charges and timing in writing.
